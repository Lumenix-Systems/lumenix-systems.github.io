About Lumenix

Lumenix is a systems-first technology foundation designed to support the next generation of interactive, immersive, and responsive digital worlds.

Rather than building a single product or experience, Lumenix focuses on the underlying systems that make believable interaction possible — across perception, space, movement, personality, and voice.

Design philosophy

Systems before content

Lumenix is built on the belief that long-term progress comes from robust systems, not handcrafted content alone. Content can be authored endlessly, but without underlying structure it remains shallow and static.

By focusing on systems, Lumenix enables experiences that can evolve, respond, and remain consistent over time — even as content changes.

Response over illusion

Many immersive experiences aim to trick the senses. Lumenix instead focuses on response — how systems sense input, interpret intent, and adapt output in ways that feel coherent and grounded.

This applies equally to physical feedback, spatial presence, character behavior, and voice interaction.

Safety as a core constraint

Safety is not treated as a post-process or optional layer. All Lumenix systems are designed with explicit boundaries, calibration, and developer control from the beginning.

The goal is not maximum stimulation, but controlled, repeatable, and responsible interaction.

The Lumenix system architecture

Each system below is modular and independently usable, but designed to interoperate through clear interfaces and shared principles.

PMPE — Personalized Multisensory Perception Engine

PMPE handles controlled modulation of physical perception. Its purpose is not to overwhelm the senses, but to subtly influence how experiences are perceived through safe, bounded feedback channels.

The system is built around strict safety constraints, user calibration, and hardware abstraction. PMPE is designed to be wearable-compatible and modular, allowing individual axes of perception to be enabled or disabled depending on context.

  • Safety-first modulation and calibration
  • Hardware-agnostic architecture
  • Designed for simulation, training, and immersive experiences

Subsystems (overview)

PMPE is composed of multiple perception-focused subsystems, each responsible for a specific modulation axis. These subsystems can operate independently or in coordinated patterns.

  • PMPE-HM (Haptic Modulation): Controlled vibration and tactile feedback designed to reinforce contact, impact, and environmental texture.
  • PMPE-TM (Thermal Modulation): Safe temperature variation used to influence perceived warmth, cold, and environmental conditions.
  • PMPE-OP (Olfactory Perception): Scent-based cues using constrained, non-hazardous delivery methods to support environmental context.
  • PMPE-GM (Gustatory Modulation): Experimental taste-related perception support, explored cautiously and always within strict safety boundaries.

Each subsystem is gated by calibration, user tolerance, and system-level safety rules.

PSCS — Personalized Spatial Computing System

PSCS is responsible for spatial coherence. It ensures that digital objects, interfaces, and interactions are anchored meaningfully within space, reducing disorientation and increasing presence.

Rather than acting as a monolithic engine, PSCS operates as a coordination layer that integrates spatial data, user context, and system outputs.

  • World-locked and context-aware spatial logic
  • XR and simulation focused design
  • Inter-system coordination

Subsystems (overview)

PSCS is structured as a spatial coordination layer rather than a single monolithic engine. Its subsystems handle different aspects of spatial understanding and presentation.

  • PSCS-VI (Visual Integration): Manages how visual elements are anchored, layered, and aligned within space.
  • PSCS-AU (Spatial Audio): Handles sound positioning, directionality, and environmental audio context.
  • PSCS-MC (Motion Context): Interprets user movement and orientation to maintain spatial stability.
  • PSCS-ER (Environment Representation): Maintains a consistent spatial model of the world and its dynamic elements.
  • PSCS-PC (Processing Core): Coordinates spatial data flow between PSCS and other Lumenix systems.

PSCS does not replace existing engines — it augments them with spatial coherence logic.

ILCS — Inertial Locomotion Control System

ILCS interprets human movement, balance, and intent to enable controlled navigation through digital spaces. It is designed to minimize discomfort while maintaining expressive movement input.

Calibration, safety limits, and adaptability are central to ILCS, making it suitable for long-duration use and varied user profiles.

  • Comfort-oriented locomotion modeling
  • Intent interpretation rather than raw input
  • Explicit safety and fallback mechanisms

Subsystems (overview)

ILCS separates raw movement input from interpreted intent, allowing locomotion to remain expressive without sacrificing comfort or safety.

  • ILCS-GAM (Gait & Acceleration Modeling): Interprets walking, running, and acceleration patterns.
  • ILCS-BSM (Balance & Stability Monitoring): Continuously evaluates balance to prevent disorientation or unsafe movement.
  • ILCS-MPCC (Motion Prediction & Correction): Anticipates user intent and smooths transitions.
  • ILCS-SCF (Safety & Constraint Framework): Enforces movement limits, fallbacks, and emergency stops.

Locomotion behavior is always bounded by explicit safety constraints.

PBES — Personality & Behavior Engine System

PBES governs how digital entities behave over time. It focuses on consistency, memory, emotional modeling, and decision-making rather than scripted reactions.

PBES is designed to give developers control over behavior boundaries while allowing characters to adapt naturally within those limits.

  • Emotion and decision modeling
  • Long-term behavioral consistency
  • Safety frameworks and developer oversight

Subsystems (overview)

PBES is divided into layered cognitive and behavioral components, allowing characters to remain consistent while still adapting over time.

  • PBES-PC (Personality Core): Defines stable personality traits and baseline tendencies.
  • PBES-EM (Emotional Model): Tracks emotional state and transitions.
  • PBES-DM (Decision Model): Governs how choices are evaluated and selected.
  • PBES-SA (Simulation & Adaptation): Allows behavior to evolve based on past interactions.
  • PBES-EX (Expression Layer): Maps internal state to visible behavior.
  • PBES-SF (Safety Framework): Ensures behavior remains within defined limits.

Developers control boundaries; PBES handles consistency within them.

VCS — Vocal Cognition System

VCS manages voice identity, speech behavior, and contextual expression. It enables spoken interaction that reflects personality, emotional state, and situational awareness.

VCS is designed to integrate tightly with PBES while remaining usable as a standalone communication layer.

  • Voice identity and variation
  • Context-aware speech behavior
  • Designed for interactive dialogue systems

Subsystems (overview)

VCS separates voice identity, speech generation, and contextual control to avoid one-size-fits-all vocal behavior.

  • VCS-ID (Voice Identity): Defines vocal characteristics and variation.
  • VCS-ASR (Speech Recognition): Interprets spoken input and extracts intent.
  • VCS-CM (Context Model): Adjusts speech based on situation, audience, and emotional state.
  • VCS-SG (Speech Generation): Produces spoken output aligned with identity and context.

VCS can operate standalone or integrate tightly with PBES for character-driven speech.

Working with Lumenix

For developers

Lumenix provides structured systems with clear boundaries. It is designed to integrate with existing engines and pipelines, not replace them.

For partners & investors

Lumenix represents a long-term platform approach. Development prioritizes architectural integrity, safety, and scalability over short-term spectacle.

For testers & collaborators

Early testing and collaboration are essential to shaping the systems. Public documentation describes intent; deeper access is shared through direct collaboration.

Public by design

Everything on this page is intentionally public. Internal tooling, prototypes, and detailed implementations are shared selectively with collaborators and testers.
